The March Compliment That Vanished by September

Why genuine feedback gets whispered in passing and dies in Slack threads—and how building continuous evidence changes a company’s entire output portfolio.

Nilesh Hiray
Founder, DAIKO • Field Edition

Field Observation: The real cost of review amnesia isn’t just awkward 1:1 meetings. It’s losing steady, high-performing contributors who never get recognized.

Key Takeaways for Leadership
1

The "Hallway Praise" Tax: 90% of real recognition happens in passing calls or Slack chats. Without a light capture layer, that proof vanishes in 48 hours.

2

Recency Bias Distorts Your Portfolio: Appraisals end up rewarding whoever solved a loud fire drill in August, while the quiet engineer who maintained stability in Q1 gets overlooked.

3

Why Forms Die in 3 Weeks: Google Forms and 10-field surveys demand too much friction. Continuous feedback only works when logging evidence takes under 10 seconds inside daily workflows.

Back in March, during a high-stakes client delivery, an engineering lead watched one of their senior developers pull off a remarkable feat. A legacy payment gateway had thrown an unhandled exception right before a release window. The developer spent seven straight hours tracing logs, patched the race condition, and kept the entire client rollout on schedule.

The manager jumped on a huddle and said: "Incredible save today. That saved this relationship."

Fast-forward six months to September appraisal calibrations. Neither the manager nor the developer could recall the incident details. The ticket had been closed, the Slack huddle had expired, and the developer’s self-review simply read: "Maintained core payment services and supported client releases."

"We aren't failing at reviews because managers are bad people. We are failing because we expect human memory to do the job of an operational ledger."

The praise was real. The business value was massive. But because it lived only as a spoken sentence, it evaporated. By review season, the developer was evaluated solely on what had happened over the last four weeks.

How Continuous Feedback Changes a Company's Output Portfolio

Most founders and executives look at continuous feedback as an HR perk—a soft "culture initiative" designed to make people feel appreciated.

That framing completely misses the point. Continuous performance bookkeeping directly shifts the commercial portfolio of the business:

01

Stops Attrition of Quiet Achievers

Your best people rarely complain—they just leave when loud, visible noise gets rewarded over disciplined, steady execution.

02

Closes Execution Drift in 7 Days

Instead of discovering a mismatched priority 6 months later during review week, course corrections happen within the same sprint.

03

Protects Client & Margin Commitments

When project leads continuously log output milestones, billable delivery risks surface early before margins get burned.

The 3 Big Myths About Continuous Feedback

When you suggest continuous feedback to engineering directors or operations heads, their guard immediately goes up. Why? Because they’ve lived through failed HR experiments before. Let’s dismantle the three biggest myths:

Myth 1: "It means scheduling weekly review meetings"

Reality: Nobody wants more calendar invites. Real continuous feedback requires zero extra meetings. It is ambient micro-logging—a 10-second verification tag attached to an already-completed pull request, client handoff, or sprint demo.

Myth 2: "It turns into an echo chamber of fake praise"

Reality: Peer "kudos" boards often devolve into popularity contests. Real governance ties feedback directly to verifiable deliverables (e.g., ticket resolution, customer SLA sign-off, or architectural review) rather than generic pats on the back.

Myth 3: "Our HRMS already has a feedback module"

Reality: Traditional HRMS portals are systems of record, not systems of execution. If an engineer has to log out of their IDE, open an HR portal, and navigate three menus just to write a note, it will never happen.

How to Overcome the Friction: The 3-Step Playbook

If Google Forms fail in three weeks and HRMS modules get ignored, how do growing teams actually solve this?

  1. Eliminate the Context Switch: Capture feedback where teams naturally talk (Slack, Teams, or issue trackers). If it takes more than two clicks, the process is dead on arrival.
  2. Enforce Objective 2-Stage Progression: Before evaluating someone for a higher band, verify that their foundational responsibilities have an unbroken trail of evidence. This removes subjective manager bias instantly.
  3. Make History Visible to the Contributor: When team members can see their log of validated contributions growing month over month, appraisal day stops being a nerve-wracking surprise—and becomes a factual calibration.
The Core Truth

If an operational process requires someone manually following up three times just to get an appraisal note or 1:1 sign-off, the problem isn't manager discipline. The operational workflow itself is broken.
